Managing Daily Responsibilities

Student life can be busy and sometimes overwhelming. Assignments, extracurriculars, and life itself can disrupt your flow and throw you off course, so learning how to manage time and responsibilities is beneficial and can make daily life feel more balanced and easier to navigate. Small changes in routine can help reduce stress and improve focus. For example, creating a simple schedule, breaking large tasks into smaller steps, and setting realistic goals can make responsibilities feel less overwhelming. It’s also important to give yourself time to rest and recharge, since constantly pushing yourself without breaks can lead to burnout. By finding a balance between productivity and self-care, students can build healthier habits that support both their academic success and overall well-being.

Helpful Tips

  1. Create a simple schedule
  2. Break large tasks into smaller steps
  3. Take regular breaks
  4. Ask for help when needed
  5. Make time for rest and relaxation

Why These Tips Help

Tip #1

A simple schedule can make it easier to navigate class assignments, upcoming tests, extracurricular activities, sporting events, and more. School can feel like scaling a mountain sometimes, but it doesn't have to feel like that. A schedule can help remind you what's happening in school on a specific day and allows you to keep all your assignments and activities organized.

Ways to Organize Your Schedule

  1. Utilize your phone calendar. All phones and computers have a calendar app/feature, so make it your best friend! All you have to do is select which day you'd like to add an event to, name the event, set the time, and your device will give you the reminders.
  2. Buy a calendar notebook/organizer. There are notebooks you can buy in-stores and online that are set up like a calendar. If you like the feeling of writing things down, then I suggest this method for you. This method is basically the same as utilizing a phone calendar, except it's in a notebook.
  3. Set alarms. In most cases, people remember tasks, just not when they need to do it. On your phone, you can set alarms for specific days, so if you're scared of forgetting to submit a completed assignment that's due at 8:00PM on Friday, set an alarm a few hours prior to the deadline so that you can check the assignment, make edits if needed, and turn it in.

Tip #2

Many assignments may seem big at first, but take a step back and break it down. Identify what small components create the overall assignment, and tackle those components one at a time. Picture this: in your English class, you have two weeks to write a 750 word essay about why or why not money is important in today's society and use five resources to support your claim. Instead of starting the overall assignment and scrambling to connect your ideas and find resources, break it up into smaller pieces. Split the tasks across days to ensure you don't miss anything and to make sure you can finish the assignment in time. Example:

  1. Do not wait until the last day to start the assignment. This only hurts you and prevents you from producing a good quality essay. Try to start the essay as soon as it's assigned. The earlier you start, the earlier you finish!
  2. Take the class or a day to think. What is your opinion? Do you think money is important in today's society? Why or why not?
  3. Depending on your opinion, take a day or two to look up websites or use real-life experiences that support your claim. Organize your notes on a document or in a notebook and be sure to write down the website name.
  4. Rather than diving right into the essay, create a layout or structure of what you want the essay to look like. Once you have a structure, create a draft that mimics what the final essay will look like. Take 2-3 days to do this.
  5. Once you have your draft, take one class period to ask your teacher and classmates for feedback. Ask 2-3 students about what they like and what they think is unclear or needs more clarity. Ask your teacher for grammatical help and confirm that ideas connect and flow smoothly. Take notes about what people say.
  6. Take 2-3 days to write the final essay. Use the same structure you created for the draft. Really focus on adding specific details and fully articulate your ideas. Don't forget to use the feedback you got from your classmates and teachers!
  7. Before you turn in the essay, reread it three times to make sure you're confident with what you have. If you'd like, I suggest asking one person to read it over to gather final opinions.

Now, your essay is ready to be submitted! Using this specific timeline would guarantee that your essay is done in 8-10 days. This means that your essay would be done 4-6 days before the deadline.

Tip #3

You know your body best. If you feel like you're having a mental fog, then step away from what you're doing for 15 minutes. During that time, you can eat or drink something, go outside for a few minutes, use the bathroom, or play a game. What matters is that you remember to return to the task at hand. It's important to understand that it's okay to admit that you need a break.You're a human, not a robot.

Tip #4

Similar to the advice in tip #3, it's okay to admit that you need help. No one is expecting you to know every small thing about a topic. If you get stuck on an assignment or just think extra help would be nice, please don't hesitate to reach out to someone. There will always be someone who is willing to help you, but you'll never know unless you ask. If one person says no, then simply ask someone else. Eventually, you'll find someone able and willing to lend you a helping hand. It's unfair for you to expect yourself to do everything alone because the stress can pile up on you and feel like a burden.

Tip #5

While it's important to stay on top of assignments and put your best effort into school work, learn how to take breaks and enjoy life. Not all assignments need or are meant to be completed in one sitting. Similar to the advice shared in tip #2, some assignments may take days or even weeks to do. If you have three assignments that need to be done, focus on one at a time. After you complete one assignment, take a break and focus your attention on something else, like watching an episode of a show you're watching or reading a book. Then, knock out the next assignment and repeat the process as many times as you need to. Don't forget to hang out with friends! Weekends are meant to give you a break from school, so utilize them. Instead of engrossing yourself in a research paper due in five days on a Saturday afternoon, make plans with friends to go to the mall or go to the beach. Spending time away from school work can clear your mind and make it easier to get work done when you return to the assignment later.
